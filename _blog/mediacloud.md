I’m currently a contributor to MediaCloud, an open-source project that began at the MIT Media Lab and is now maintained by a consortium of universities and nonprofit organizations.

MediaCloud is, at its core, a large-scale data source for media research. The system continuously ingests news stories from across the web into an Elasticsearch cluster, making them searchable and analyzable through a suite of web-based research tools. The database includes stories dating back to 2008 and currently ingests around half a million new stories every day.

I joined the team in 2022 as a data engineer, building internal tools to run NLP analyses across our dataset. Over time, I became a core contributor to a major redesign of the system—helping to transform what had once been a sprawling monolith into a lean, modular, and maintainable piece of research infrastructure. That work made it easier for other teams to integrate their own analysis pipelines and for researchers to experiment with new kinds of questions at scale.

In the summer of 2024, I stepped into the role of product manager. During my first year in that position, I helped scope and deploy a major hardware upgrade to our Elasticsearch backend. The upgrade improved query performance by nearly twenty-fold and secured our data storage capacity for the next several years. It also opened the door to new collaborations with academic and nonprofit partners who rely on MediaCloud to study information ecosystems—from journalism and public health to climate communication.

MediaCloud continues to grow as a shared resource for researchers, journalists, and policy analysts. I’m proud to have helped shape its evolution into a faster, more open, and more sustainable platform for understanding how the news moves through the world.
